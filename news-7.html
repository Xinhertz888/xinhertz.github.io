ByteDance Reportedly Nears Deal for MediaTek's Custom AI ASICs

Bloomberg reported on Oct 14, citing people familiar with the matter, that ByteDance is close to reaching an agreement to buy customized data-center semiconductors from MediaTek. The ASICs would be tailored for ByteDance's platforms, with the purchase quantity undisclosed; if the deal closes, ByteDance could become MediaTek's second-largest customer for this class of chips after Google.

Neither company confirmed the talks: ByteDance did not immediately comment, and MediaTek declined to comment. The report also said MediaTek expects about $2 billion in AI-chip sales in 2026 and aims to capture 15% of an estimated $80 billion market next year.

The deal would land as ByteDance ramps AI spending — its 2026 capital expenditure is estimated at more than 200 billion yuan (about $29.4 billion), with roughly half earmarked for advanced semiconductors, alongside plans for test orders of 20,000 Nvidia H200 GPUs at roughly $20,000 apiece.

ASML Studies Further 2028 Capacity Boost as Q3 Sales Hit Record €11.7 Billion

ASML posted record quarterly net sales of €11.7 billion for Q3 2026 on Oct 14 — up 25% from Q2's €9.3 billion and 55% from a year earlier — with a gross margin of 57.5% and net income of €5 billion. The company guided full-year 2026 net sales to €44–45 billion, about 36% higher than 2025, at roughly 56% gross margin, and said China should account for about 20% of its business.

CEO Christophe Fouquet said the company's planned capacity for 2027 and 2028 still cannot fully satisfy customer demand, so ASML is studying a further expansion of 2028 output. The company expects to deliver about 65 EUV systems in 2026 — more than 50% above last year — while immersion DUV shipments stay roughly flat.

Fouquet added that customers see the supply shortage lasting at least through 2028: new orders for 2028 have jumped sharply, and some customers have already opened talks for 2029 and 2030. He also confirmed that TSMC and ASML broadly agree on introducing High-NA EUV into volume production around 2030.

Guangzhou Foundry CanSemi Jumps 274.69% on ChiNext Debut

CanSemi (粤芯半导体), dubbed "Guangzhou's No. 1 chip company," listed on the Shenzhen ChiNext board on Oct 13 — the first pure wafer-manufacturing company on the board. It priced at 12.01 yuan per share, issued 513 million shares and raised 6.157 billion yuan gross, then opened at 45 yuan per share — up 274.69% — for a market capitalization of about 130 billion yuan.

Founded in December 2017 in Guangzhou's Huangpu district, the company is the Greater Bay Area's first 12-inch wafer maker in volume production. It focuses on specialty analog and mixed-signal processes from 180nm to 55nm — MS, HV, CIS, BCD, eNVM, MOSFET, IGBT and SiPho — and, per Frost & Sullivan, is mainland China's only company with mass-production capability on a 12-inch silicon-photonics platform, with more than 7,000 SiPho wafers taped out by September 15, 2026.

Its first two fabs are planned for 80,000 wafers per month (63,300 reached by end-2025) and should approach 120,000 wpm after the phase-3 project. 2025 capacity utilization ran at 96.38% with a 99.47% sell-through rate. Revenue grew from 1.044 billion yuan in 2023 to 2.582 billion in 2025 (a 57.3% CAGR), but cumulative three-year losses exceeded 6.5 billion yuan, and the company targets breakeven no earlier than 2029.

Silicon-Photonics Maker Xihe Clears SSE Review, Seeks 1.83 Billion Yuan IPO

Shanghai Xihe Technology Co., Ltd. (羊禾科技) cleared its SSE review on Oct 14, according to the exchange's website. The national "little giant" plans to raise 1.83 billion yuan to industrialize silicon-photonic ICs for AI compute and data centers, develop next-generation chips and build an R&D center.

Founded in 2021 in Shanghai's Putuo district, Xihe makes high-performance silicon-photonic integrated chips covering 400G, 800G and 1.6T rates, and is expanding into 3.2T and 6.4T NPO/CPO transceiver chips. Its products sit in the supply chains of major international optical-module makers and are deployed in AI clusters and hyperscale data centers.

Cumulative shipments topped 12 million chips by August 2026 — one of very few vendors worldwide at that scale — and Frost & Sullivan puts its 2025 global market share at about 13%. The company held 3.279 billion yuan in backorders at the end of H1 2026, with 800G products making up 47.57%.

SIA Report: America's 95% "Safe" Chip Supply Chain Is a Mirage

The U.S. Semiconductor Industry Association, together with McKinsey, has published a new report, "Understanding Dependencies in the U.S. Semiconductor Supply Chain," that punctures the industry's sense of security. By company headquarters, 95% of the roughly $27 billion in equipment and materials the U.S. chip ecosystem consumes each year comes from suppliers based in the U.S., Europe or Japan — but measured by actual production location, that share falls to 68%, with the portion made in mainland China, Israel, Southeast Asia, South Korea and Taiwan jumping from 5% to 32%.

The report rates about 11% of Tier 1 supply categories as "high vulnerability" and 38% as medium — roughly half in total. And once sub-tier dependencies are included, more than three-quarters of Tier 1 categories depend on at least one high-risk upstream input.

Stark examples: mainland China supplies about 40% of the world's xenon and krypton (critical for lithography) and about 35% of tungsten, plus roughly 95% of gallium and electronic-grade polysilicon; Japan provides about 75% of ABF substrates and 70% of mask blanks. The report urges targeted incentives, strategic stockpiles and deeper coordination with allied supply chains rather than wholesale reshoring.
