China's Chip Metrology Market Tops RMB 48 Billion as Local Players Chase Sub-15% Share

Mainland China's semiconductor metrology and inspection equipment market has surpassed RMB 48 billion and is growing at a compound rate above 17.5%, yet domestic vendors hold less than 15% — and under 5% in the ultra-high-end segment — leaving what the industry calls one of the deepest localization gaps in the chip supply chain.

Global concentration remains extreme: in 2025 KLA held about 53% of the inspection-systems market, followed by Applied Materials (~15%), ASML (~11%), Hitachi High-Tech (~7.8%) and Tokyo Electron (~5%), with the top five taking 91.8% combined; KLA alone holds close to 60% of the China market. Electron-beam inspection is a roughly US$3.3 billion global market, while advanced-packaging inspection and metrology is about US$925 million.

Suzhou-based startup Precise (普雷赛斯) just closed a Series A round on the order of RMB 100 million backed by investors including E-Town Capital, betting on fully in-house development — its core modules exceed 95% self-developed, spanning optical sensing to AI agents — and focusing on advanced-node and advanced-packaging inspection for TSV, RDL, Bump and HBM processes.

Chinese SiC Makers Push Toward 12- and 14-Inch Wafers

Four Chinese players have disclosed parallel breakthroughs on oversized silicon carbide, pushing from today's 8-inch ramp straight toward 12- and 14-inch formats: a 12-inch wafer yields about 4.4 times as many chips as a 6-inch one, making size the industry's next cost lever.

Hantian Tiancheng (瀚天天成) reported 12-inch (300mm) n-type 4H-SiC epitaxy with thickness non-uniformity improved from 7.68% to 1.72% and doping non-uniformity from 14.62% to 2.15%, reaching 8-inch-equivalent quality and securing small-batch orders. Shanxi Tiancheng (山西天成) demonstrated a 14-inch SiC single crystal with 30mm effective thickness, 90% localized equipment parts, 60 homegrown growth furnaces in phase one, and a phase-two target of 2028 with about RMB 800 million in annual output.

Lijing Xinke (立晶芯科) has developed an AI-assisted 12-inch crystal-growth system — after its first 8-inch PVT ingot in January 2025 and first liquid-phase ingot in June — and expects a high-quality 12-inch single crystal within two months, with pilot samples next year and mass production the year after. A Zhejiang University team separately produced a ~360mm (about 14-inch) polycrystalline SiC ingot by PVT, reporting room-temperature thermal conductivity of about 323.8 W/(m·K).

Lithography Startup Milai Xincheng Raises RMB 361 Million

Shanghai-based Milai Xincheng Semiconductor (米莱芯程), a projection-exposure (lithography) equipment maker founded in Pudong in March 2025, is raising RMB 361 million in new capital at a RMB 1.5 billion pre-money valuation, with listed investor United Chemical (联合化学) contributing RMB 60 million.

After the round, United Chemical's stake will be diluted from 37.28% to 33.27%. It is the company's third backing from United Chemical, following RMB 120 million in July 2025 (RMB 500 million valuation) and RMB 200 million in November 2025 (RMB 700 million valuation).

Founder and CTO Gao An previously worked at ASML on DUV and EUV projection lithography. The company posted 2025 revenue of RMB 40.48 million with a net loss of RMB 71.84 million, and first-half 2026 revenue of RMB 8.01 million with a RMB 61.59 million loss.

Auto Industry Faces Cost 'High Wall' as Memory Prices Spike Post-Holiday

China's auto supply chain is facing across-the-board cost hikes after the Golden Week holiday: more than 60 tire makers issued over 70 price-increase letters since September (2%-5%), carbon black prices rose about 45% within a single month, and rubber futures hit a nine-year high.

Memory chips are automakers' biggest pain point: TrendForce reports global conventional DRAM contract prices rose 90%-95% quarter-on-quarter in Q1 2026, while automotive-grade storage purchase prices jumped about 180% between March and June as AI demand crowds out automotive memory capacity.

UBS estimates memory inflation alone adds RMB 3,000-7,000 to the cost of a high-end ADAS vehicle; NIO said its per-vehicle cost rose roughly RMB 14,000 in Q2 versus end-2025, with memory accounting for about RMB 10,000 of that.

Automakers are fighting back with long-term agreements (GM and Ford both signed LTAs with Micron in July), multi-source battery strategies, and material substitution such as aluminum-for-copper; industry profit still fell 16% year-on-year in January-August with a sales margin of just 3.6%.

The article notes China's automotive chip localization rate topped roughly 35% in the first half of 2026, but high-end compute SoCs and high-safety MCUs remain under 5% localized.

Domestic Brands Take Over 60% of China's Servo Market as Industrial 'Chip' Momentum Builds

China's 2025 general servo market reached RMB 22.96 billion, with domestic brands topping 60% share for the first time (versus under 50% five years ago), while Japanese brands fell from over 50% before 2020 to about 26%, according to GeWu Zhisheng's market white paper.

Low-voltage inverters tell a similar story: the 2025 market was about RMB 29.27 billion, with local brands climbing to 46% and breaking the long-standing ABB/Siemens dominance.

Invosun (汇川技术) holds roughly 22% servo share as the domestic No.1, ahead of Panasonic and Yaskawa, while StarPower Semiconductor earned Invosun's 'strategic supplier' honor in January 2026 with custom-developed IGBT and SiC modules.

Local power-device makers are evolving from build-to-spec suppliers into co-definition partners: Silan Micro is advancing an 'MCU + power module + driver IC' integrated portfolio, and MacroMicro is building a 'chip + module' platform across new energy, EVs, and data centers.

Caveats remain: high-end IGBT modules, precision SiC devices, and high-end IPM modules still lag by a generation and depend heavily on imports, while wide-bandgap cost barriers are slowing large-scale penetration.
