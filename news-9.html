Changfei Advanced Discloses Diffusion-Bonded SiC Modules, A3-Grade Fab at Baychip Expo

At the 2026 Bay Area Semiconductor Industry Ecosystem Expo (Baychip Expo) in Shenzhen, SiC power semiconductor maker Changfei Advanced disclosed fresh progress on high-performance SiC MOSFETs, PCB-embedded packaging and its A3-grade wafer fab, in an interview with Li Gangqun, vice president of its strategic operations center.

The EZ family is China's first power module line built on an advanced diffusion-bonding process, targeting AI data centers (AIDC) and solid-state transformers (SST). Diffusion-bonded joints deliver 2–3x higher bonding strength; the EZ2C cuts junction-to-case thermal resistance by 18% and full-load die junction temperature by more than 25°C, while supporting operation above 200°C. The EZ3C uses an efficient SHB three-level topology to push a single SST module above 100 kW, halving the cascade stages of a 10 kV AI SST system from 16 to 8.

On trench devices, the company started its trench MOSFET patent program in 2023; double-side deep masking/burial techniques plus rounded trench-bottom processing cut trench sidewall roughness (Ra) to 0.15 nm versus 0.7 nm for rivals. As of October 2025 it held 79 public patents in the field, ranking third worldwide; first- and second-generation devices are designated TG1N and TG2N.

Its Wuhan base is the first SiC wafer fab in China to deploy an A3-grade AMHS overhead-transport and dispatch system, with an automatic wafer-start rate above 98% and smart dispatch above 90%. CP yield on 5 mm × 5 mm automotive-grade large dice is stable above 98%, new-product tape-in cycles are 3x faster, and an AI-driven fab utilities system cut overall fab energy use by more than 10.5%.

Zhiyuan Robotics Takes Strategic Stake in Tsinghua-Backed RISC-V AI Chip Startup

Embodied-intelligence leader Zhiyuan Robotics has taken a strategic stake in Xinji Wuxian (芯际无限), the only RISC-V AI chip company incubated with technology transfer and equity from Tsinghua University. Founded in August 2023, the startup covers the full humanoid-robot stack, from end-effector and motion control to cerebellum and brain-level decision chips.

Its XC64AE series is purpose-built for embodied-intelligence scenarios, with the second-generation XC64AE family slated to complete tape-out in 2027. On the automotive side, the XC32MC body-motor-control MCU is already in mass production in vehicles with aggregate orders exceeding 5 million units; the XC32LS meets the ASIL-D functional-safety grade, recently taped out and powered on, and is now in testing with multiple automakers and Tier-1 suppliers ahead of 2027 mass production; the XC32DC central domain controller integrates an in-house NPU.

The coverage notes that traditional ARM-based automotive chips can carry combined licensing costs of 15–20% of product cost, and that Tsinghua Huakong Technology Transfer Co. took a strategic stake in 2026 via core RISC-V IP. The team is led by founder and CEO Cheng Baozhong and CTO Wei Jian — formerly of Qualcomm, where he led the first in-house GPU and taped out ASIL-D RISC-V cores and automotive cockpit chips.

Zhiyuan, backed by investors including Huawei and Tencent, began its Hong Kong IPO process in July with a targeted valuation of 40–50 billion yuan.

Dell CEO Warns AI Server Memory Shortage to Worsen Into 2027

Dell Technologies CEO Michael Dell has warned that tight supply of HBM and DRAM will worsen further in 2027, as AI infrastructure demand keeps outpacing global memory capacity growth — making memory the key bottleneck for the AI server industry.

Dell currently sits on a record $95 billion backlog of enterprise and AI server orders, but converting those orders into revenue hinges on securing enough memory chips.

According to The Wall Street Journal, Dell believes the supply constraint could last up to four years, since new fabs take years from planning to full production — much of the added capacity is not expected to be fully online until 2029 or 2030.

To limit risk, Dell runs a lean-inventory model — buying DRAM close to final assembly to avoid tying up working capital — while locking in supply through long-term vendor agreements and customer prepayments.

Rising memory prices are also pushing up manufacturing costs for AI servers and other high-end systems, adding pressure across the AI supply chain.

XHEART Closes Strategic Round, Lifetime Funding Reaches ~5 Billion Yuan

Chinese physical-AI compute chip startup XHEART (新芯航途) completed a strategic funding round on October 16, bringing its cumulative financing to approximately 5 billion yuan.

Renault Group joined as a first-time investor, Chery Auto increased its stake, and domestic memory leader CXMT (Changxin Memory) also participated in the round.

XHEART builds dedicated compute for edge-side physical-AI models. Its flagship product, the X7 ADAS large-model chip launched in March, uses a dedicated NPU with a hardware-software co-design architecture targeting 200+ TOPS for L2++ urban driving assistance and L3/L4 autonomous driving.

The X7 debuted on the SAIC Volkswagen ID.ERA 9X and is entering the supply systems of SAIC's MG and Roewe brands, GM's Cadillac, and selected Chery models. The company's largest shareholder traces back to Momenta founder Cao Xudong.

VTest Wins Approval for 2 Billion Yuan Convertible Bond to Expand High-End Chip Test Capacity

Shanghai VTest Semiconductor (伟测科技) has received SSE approval to issue up to 2 billion yuan in convertible bonds, with proceeds earmarked for expanding test capacity for AI compute chips, memory chips, and high-end CIS.

The funds target two projects: the Shanghai HQ base (700 million yuan of the raise) and the Southwest HQ and R&D test base in Chengdu (800 million yuan), both involving new land, facilities, and high-end test equipment.

VTest, a leading third-party IC test house, covers wafer test, final test, SLT, and burn-in for AI chips, CPUs, GPUs, DPUs, SoCs, FPGAs, MCUs, memory, RF, and high-end CIS devices, serving over 200 customers including Unisoc, ZTE Microelectronics, GigaDevice, Fudan Microelectronics, SMIC, Rockchip, and Novosense.

The company reported H1 2026 revenue of 1.072 billion yuan, up 69% year on year, with net profit of 159 million yuan, up 57.6%, driven by high-end test demand from AI compute, smart driving, and large-capacity memory chips.

This is VTest's second straight year of convertible-bond financing, following an 1.175 billion yuan issue in April 2025 that was redeemed in March 2026 after a share-price rally.
