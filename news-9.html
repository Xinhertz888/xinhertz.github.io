Xianyi Micro Closes Series A to Scale Magnetic-Isolation Chips, Sets Up East China HQ in Wuxi

Shenzhen-based Xianyi Microelectronics, which says it is China's first commercial magnetic-isolation chip company, has closed a Series A round worth tens of millions of yuan led by Xizhou International, and established its East China headquarters in Binhu, Wuxi.

The company says it built China's first commercial magnetic-isolation process line and now offers nearly 300 magnetic-isolation products across signal, interface, driver and power lines, with automotive products AEC-Q100 qualified and in volume supply for industrial and energy-storage applications.

The Wuxi base will serve as its East China R&D and support center, tapping the Yangtze River Delta customer cluster as demand grows in AI compute power infrastructure, solid-state transformers and embodied intelligence — areas where high-voltage isolation requirements are intensifying.

Four SiC Projects in China Accelerate Toward Production

Four silicon carbide projects across China reported fresh progress this week, signaling continued capacity expansion in the third-generation semiconductor race.

Lips' Yangzhou SiC project, a provincial major project with total investment of 1 billion yuan (180 million yuan in phase one), will deliver its first phase — two automotive-grade SiC module packaging and testing lines with annual capacity of 1.5 million units — by year-end 2026; full build-out targets 3 million units per year.

Galaxy Microelectronics topped out the main structure of its Changzhou base on October 8 and expects trial production in Q2 next year; at full capacity it will produce 50 million IGBT and SiC power modules annually.

Xinjia Electronics said its fully automated automotive-grade SiC module line is already running at full load with 600,000 units of annual capacity, with products qualified into supply chains of leading domestic automakers; two more lines planned for next year would lift capacity to 1.5 million automotive-grade and 4 million industrial-grade units per year by late 2027 or early 2028.

Zhongyi Chuangxin reported its optical-grade SiC powder project — supplying semi-insulating SiC powder for AR glasses lenses — is 80% complete since construction began in June.

Global AI Compute Market Nears US$1.2 Trillion in 2026, Industry Data Shows

Industry research reports cited in the coverage put global computing capacity at 4,495 EFlops as of June 2025, up 117% year on year, with intelligent computing accounting for 85% of the total — marking a structural shift from general to intelligent computing.

The global AI compute market is approaching US$1.2 trillion in 2026; the AI semiconductor market is estimated at about US$485 billion this year, on track for roughly US$753 billion by 2030, while global semiconductor spending hit US$793 billion in 2025, up 21%.

Capital is flooding in: global data center capex rose 57% in 2025 and is expected to exceed US$1 trillion in 2026, driven by more than 10 million high-end accelerators, with AI infrastructure spending already surpassing US$1.3 trillion.

China's computing capacity reached 962 EFlops — about 21% of the global total, up 73% year on year — and domestic AI accelerator shipments keep climbing: Nvidia holds about 55% of China's AI card market (2.2 million units), domestic vendors a combined 41% (1.65 million), and AMD and others about 4% of a roughly 4-million-unit total.

Sources: eefocus.com

TSMC Taps GlobalFoundries in US$2 Billion Silicon Interposer Deal for CoWoS

GlobalFoundries announced on October 8 a multi-year manufacturing agreement with TSMC: GF will expand silicon interposer capacity at its Malta, New York fab to supply TSMC's CoWoS advanced-packaging ecosystem, in a deal worth an estimated US$2 billion over an initial five-year term, with volume ramp expected in the first half of 2028.

The deal makes GF the first US-based supplier of silicon interposers for advanced packaging, adding a clear supply-chain regionalization dimension to what is otherwise a pure capacity play.

Counterpoint Research estimates TSMC's CoWoS supply-demand gap at about 20% in 2026, narrowing to roughly 10% in 2027 as internal capacity expands toward 185,000 wafers per month; TrendForce expects TSMC's 2026 average monthly CoWoS capacity at 120,000–140,000 wafers — a record — with 2027 capacity up more than 60%.

Yole Group data cited in the coverage puts the global advanced-packaging market at about US$44.3 billion in 2022, growing to roughly US$78.6 billion by 2028 at a compound annual rate of around 10%, with AI demand as the core driver.

Sources: laoyaoba.com

Cirrus Logic Named as Rival Bidder for Synaptics as onsemi Switches to All-Cash Offer

The mystery "Party A" in Synaptics' October 8 proxy filing is Cirrus Logic, which made an unsolicited rival bid in September after onsemi's approach, Bloomberg reported citing people familiar with the matter.

onsemi revised its proposal on October 1 to an all-cash transaction worth about US$5.7 billion (US$123 per share), replacing its original all-stock offer valued at roughly US$7 billion.
