Japan Approves TSMC's 3nm Plan for Kumamoto Fab Two, Targeting 6,000 Wafers a Month

Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry on October 16 approved TSMC's plan to produce 3nm advanced semiconductors at its second Kumamoto fab, which would be the first 3nm chip manufacturing on Japanese soil.

The fab was originally slated for 6nm and 12nm processes at 48,000 wafers per month plus 40nm capacity; the new plan sets 3nm output at 6,000 wafers per month, with METI saying the sharp capacity cut reflects the shift to higher-performance products. The approval covers the fab's first phase, and TSMC plans to add capacity with further government support.

METI also approved changes at the operating first Kumamoto fab: 12–16nm production will be discontinued, and 22–28nm capacity will be trimmed from 55,000 to 45,000 wafers per month.

Japanese media report the switch to 3nm reflects rising AI demand, and Nikkei confirmed the new 3nm plant has been approved for subsidies.

Shanghai OSAT Xiangxin Integrated Circuits Declared Bankrupt

The Shanghai Third Intermediate People's Court on October 15 declared Shanghai Xiangxin Integrated Circuits Co. bankrupt (case (2025) Hu 03 Po No. 918), ruling the company cannot pay its due debts and its assets are insufficient to cover them; the court had accepted the bankruptcy liquidation application on September 19, 2025.

Founded in November 2011, Xiangxin focused on power-management IC packaging and testing, building SOP and SOT lines covering wafer thinning, dicing, wire bonding, molding, metal surface treatment, trimming and final test, with total investment of RMB 60 million.

Its packaging services supplied chipmakers including Wuxi Chipown, with products going into consumer electronics and industrial control applications.

The ruling adds another bankruptcy case to China's intensely competitive OSAT sector, where many smaller packagers are under pressure.

Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan: US-China Chip Gap "Not That Big"

In a Tokyo interview in early October, Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan said the US government's minority stake in Intel has no impact on the company's governance and does not affect its commercial strategy, and that Intel's restructuring is 80% complete.

He credited government backing for enabling long-term investment in a competitive foundry business, citing TSMC's decade-plus journey to chipmaking leadership.

On China's semiconductor progress, Tan said US and Chinese firms "each have their own strengths and weaknesses," and when asked whether Chinese chipmaking could catch up within five to ten years, replied: "as things stand, I don't think the gap is that big."

Huawei: 2027 Kirin Chip in Testing, Results "As Expected"

At a Kirin chip technology briefing at Huawei's Shanghai Lianqihu R&D center on October 13 — its first dedicated Kirin event in six years — Huawei said it systematically reviewed the mass-production progress of its "logic folding" technology.

The company also disclosed that its next-generation Kirin chip, expected in 2027, has entered testing, with results meeting expectations.

China's Equipment Trio Rushes Into Hybrid Bonding: Tuojing's RMB 2.5 Billion Deal Leads

On the evening of October 15, Tuojing Technology and NAURA both disclosed acquisition plans targeting hybrid bonding equipment; three days earlier, AMEC's subsidiary announced a capital injection of up to RMB 35 million into bonding firm Qinghe Jingyuan — three major domestic chip-equipment makers moving on the same track within days.

Tuojing Technology plans to acquire 82.97% of Wuxi Shangji plus 100% each of Wuxi Kuanghang and Shanghai Tainawei for RMB 2.5 billion via shares and cash, raising up to RMB 1.82 billion in supporting funds alongside. Wuxi Shangji, a PVD and etching equipment maker, committed to revenues of no less than RMB 350 million, 450 million and 610 million for 2026–2028. After the deal, Tuojing will offer a complete 3D-integration equipment product line covering thin-film deposition, etching, bonding and metrology, enabling one-stop procurement and joint process development for customers.

NAURA's subsidiary will acquire hybrid-bonding equipment maker Nuohe Jianwei for RMB 405 million in cash. Nuohe's tools have already reached nanometer-level alignment precision, secured repeat orders, and are awaiting validation by leading customers.

Hybrid bonding, called the "chokepoint process" of 3D chips, is entering a fast landing phase, with related equipment demand expected to multiply by 2030, driven by the explosive growth of AI/HPC and HBM. Dutch firm BESI still holds about 70% of the global market, but Chinese equipment makers are accelerating their catch-up from zero to one, shifting competition from single-tool breakthroughs to platform-based turnkey solutions.
