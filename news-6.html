NXP Ara240 Edge AI Processor Hits Mouser Shelves

NXP's Ara240, a discrete neural-network processor for the edge delivering up to 40 eTOPS, enables local inference of LLMs and vision-language models without cloud dependency.

Mouser began stocking the part on October 5. The chip pairs with 16 GB of LPDDR4, connects via PCIe Gen4 and USB 3.2, and includes secure boot with a hardware root of trust.

Sources: Mouser Electronics

AMD Plans Substantial Supply Increase in 2027, CEO Says

AMD CEO Lisa Su said in Taipei on October 6 that demand has outstripped supply and the company plans to substantially increase chip supply in 2027.

The Asia trip is capacity-focused: meetings with Foxconn and TSMC in Taiwan, plus planned talks with Samsung and SK Hynix in Korea on memory supply.

Sources: Reuters

ST Adds Dual-Accelerometer 6-Axis IMUs

ST launched two 6-axis IMUs with dual accelerometers in a single package: the consumer-grade LSM6DSK320X and the industrial-grade ISM6HGK256X.

A symmetric mirrored gyroscope structure cancels vibration and shock, while the dual accelerometers capture micro-motion and high-g events simultaneously without range switching. Ranges span ±16g to ±320g, the industrial part is rated from −40°C to +105°C, and adapter boards ship from November 2026.

Sources: EDN Asia

Taiwan Indicts 10 for Relabeling US-Controlled Chips for Resale to China

Taiwan prosecutors indicted ten people for buying controlled chips from Analog Devices and Texas Instruments under the guise of a Taiwanese research institute since 2017, relabeling chips made in overseas factories as "Made in Taiwan" and reselling them to China via Hong Kong.

Prosecutors are seeking a 10-year sentence for the ringleader; illicit profits totaled NT$300 million (about US$9.4 million).

Sources: GMA News

Samsung's Memory Margins Top Nvidia's as Q3 Profit Hits Record

Samsung's preliminary third-quarter results set a record at 107.4 trillion won, driven almost entirely by the memory business, with memory margins reportedly exceeding Nvidia's.

HBM is consuming a disproportionate share of wafer capacity, and brokerages expect the rally in conventional memory to extend into next year.

Sources: SE Daily
