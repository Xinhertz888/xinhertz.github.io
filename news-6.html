China's DF1000 AI Chip Adapts 130B-Parameter Model Training Pipeline in 7 Days

Domestic AI-chip startup Dongfang Suanxin said its DF1000 processor, launched in July, completed a full bring-up from chip to cluster in a short period and adapted the training pipeline of the Step 130B large model in just 7 days — clarified as getting operators, distributed links, and cluster scheduling working so training could start, not a complete from-scratch training run of the 130B model.

The DF1000 uses a software-defined chip architecture whose compute arrays and interconnect can be dynamically switched between data movement, tensor, and vector compute without a respin, and a 3D hybrid-bonded near-memory compute design with logic and DRAM wafers vertically stacked, copper-to-copper direct bonding, and sub-micron interconnect pitch.

Measured figures: 520 TFLOPS of BF16 compute at 14nm, 6.4 TB/s memory bandwidth, and 900 GB/s scale-up interconnect bandwidth — over 5x the memory bandwidth of a conventional HBM setup at the same capacity. The software stack supports PyTorch, Megatron-LM, vLLM, Triton, Kubernetes, Hugging Face, and SGLang, with LLaMA, DeepSeek, and Qwen models claimed to work out of the box.

VP Guo Wei said at the DF1000 launch that the chip's goal is to fix real bottlenecks in large-model training and inference, not to look good on a few spec parameters.

China's FPGA Vendors Push High-End Launches in 2026, Review Says

Chinese FPGA vendors are moving from entry-level "drop-in replacements" to original high-end innovation in 2026, according to an October 18 industry review on eefocus, which points to four shifts: Physical AI going mainstream, FPGA Agent intelligent development, Chiplet packaging breakthroughs, and volume ramp of domestic high-end products.

In high-end compute, Pango Microsystems' Titan-3 series is described as China's top mass-produced general FPGA, delivering 1,300K LUTs of logic capacity with DDR5 and PCIe 3.0, targeting 5G/6G communications, data-center acceleration, and high-speed image codecs. For edge AI, Fudan Microelectronics' new AI heterogeneous computing platform combines CPU, FPGA, and an in-house NPU for the edge deployment of 70B-class models, with its FMZQ400TAI part rated at 128 TOPS INT8 peak.

In automotive, Anlogic's Phoenix PH1P series passed AEC-Q100 Grade 2 qualification, among the few domestic high-end FPGAs that can ship in cars in volume. Efinix's 16nm Titanium series pairs a Quantum variable-logic architecture with PCIe Gen4 and 16 Gbps SerDes for low-power, high-speed interconnect such as optical communications and high-speed backplanes.

The review also notes smarter domestic EDA tooling lowering the development barrier: Pango's PDS tool is positioned against Xilinx Vivado with SystemVerilog support, and all major domestic vendors now ship AI-assisted development flows for auto-routing, timing closure, and bug triage.

Sources: eefocus.com

Japan Approves TSMC's 3nm Plan for Kumamoto Fab Two, Targeting 6,000 Wafers a Month

Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry on October 16 approved TSMC's plan to produce 3nm advanced semiconductors at its second Kumamoto fab, which would be the first 3nm chip manufacturing on Japanese soil.

The fab was originally slated for 6nm and 12nm processes at 48,000 wafers per month plus 40nm capacity; the new plan sets 3nm output at 6,000 wafers per month, with METI saying the sharp capacity cut reflects the shift to higher-performance products. The approval covers the fab's first phase, and TSMC plans to add capacity with further government support.

METI also approved changes at the operating first Kumamoto fab: 12–16nm production will be discontinued, and 22–28nm capacity will be trimmed from 55,000 to 45,000 wafers per month.

Japanese media report the switch to 3nm reflects rising AI demand, and Nikkei confirmed the new 3nm plant has been approved for subsidies.

Shanghai OSAT Xiangxin Integrated Circuits Declared Bankrupt

The Shanghai Third Intermediate People's Court on October 15 declared Shanghai Xiangxin Integrated Circuits Co. bankrupt (case (2025) Hu 03 Po No. 918), ruling the company cannot pay its due debts and its assets are insufficient to cover them; the court had accepted the bankruptcy liquidation application on September 19, 2025.

Founded in November 2011, Xiangxin focused on power-management IC packaging and testing, building SOP and SOT lines covering wafer thinning, dicing, wire bonding, molding, metal surface treatment, trimming and final test, with total investment of RMB 60 million.

Its packaging services supplied chipmakers including Wuxi Chipown, with products going into consumer electronics and industrial control applications.

The ruling adds another bankruptcy case to China's intensely competitive OSAT sector, where many smaller packagers are under pressure.

Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan: US-China Chip Gap "Not That Big"

In a Tokyo interview in early October, Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan said the US government's minority stake in Intel has no impact on the company's governance and does not affect its commercial strategy, and that Intel's restructuring is 80% complete.

He credited government backing for enabling long-term investment in a competitive foundry business, citing TSMC's decade-plus journey to chipmaking leadership.

On China's semiconductor progress, Tan said US and Chinese firms "each have their own strengths and weaknesses," and when asked whether Chinese chipmaking could catch up within five to ten years, replied: "as things stand, I don't think the gap is that big."
