FCC to Vote on Barring Chinese Labs from Testing US Electronics

The US Federal Communications Commission announced it will hold a final vote on October 29 on its 'third report and order.' If passed, testing labs and telecom certification bodies (TCBs) located in countries without a mutual recognition agreement (MRA) with the US would lose FCC recognition starting December 1, 2028 — and mainland China is not on the reciprocal list.

FCC chair Brendan Carr said 82% of electronic devices authorized for sale in the US in 2025 were tested in China, with under 4% tested in US labs. The rule is drawn by lab geography, not ownership: about 27 labs in China belonging to Intertek, SGS, TÜV Rheinland and Bureau Veritas would be affected.

Of the 609 FCC-recognized test labs, mainland China accounts for 25.1%, Taiwan 18.4% and the US 17.9%. The draft lists more than 60 'reciprocal economies' including Taiwan, Hong Kong, Japan, South Korea, Vietnam and Singapore; mainland China and India are excluded.

The FCC estimates the industry faces about $23.6 million in one-time conversion costs and roughly $79.3 million in added annual testing expenses — FCC certification tests cost $400 to $1,300 in China versus $3,000 to $4,000 in the US — while about 35% of the $57.6 million in annual fees paid to non-reciprocal labs would flow back to US labs.

China has already retaliated: on August 5 its commerce ministry added US-based Compliance Testing LLC to its countermeasure list, and barred US certification bodies from CCC factory inspections. Listed Chinese lab operator CTI said the two-year transition will hit domestic labs but the business is a small share of its revenue.

Allwinner Showcases Industrial Chip Lineup at CIIF 2026

Allwinner Technology presented its full industrial chip portfolio at the 26th China International Industry Fair, which opened October 12 at Shanghai's National Exhibition and Convention Center, covering six scenarios: PLC, HMI, gateways, power and energy, robotics and industrial vision. Its T153 chip won the fair's 'Guoxin Innovation Achievement Award.'

Four core chips anchor the lineup. The T153 combines a quad-core Arm Cortex-A7 with a single-core RISC-V XuanTie E907, supports DDR3/DDR3L/DDR4, three Gigabit Ethernet ports, two CAN-FD channels and 24-channel GPADC, and targets small and medium PLCs. The T536 pairs a quad-core Cortex-A55 with a 3 TOPS NPU, adds four CAN-FD, 17 UARTs, PCIe and dual Gigabit Ethernet with full-link ECC, aimed at power, energy and industrial control.

The T736 combines dual Cortex-A76 and hexa-core Cortex-A55 CPUs with a PowerVR GPU and 3–4 TOPS NPU, PCIe 3.0, 8K decoding and 4K encoding for edge AI and robotics. The V883 uses a quad-core RISC-V CPU with a 4 TOPS NPU, 4K@75fps encoding, 4K@60fps decoding and up to eight sensor inputs for industrial vision.

More than 14 ecosystem partners exhibited alongside Allwinner, with over 50 industrial boards on display. The company also said it has built a CRA compliance and security service system following IEC 62443-4-1 in development and ISO/IEC 30111 and 29147 for vulnerability response.

CanSemi Lists on ChiNext as Guangdong's First 12-Inch Fab, Shares Jump 275% on Debut

CanSemi, Guangzhou's 12-inch specialty foundry, debuted on Shenzhen's ChiNext board on October 13, with shares opening 275% above the 12.01-yuan issue price at 45.00 yuan, valuing the company at 129.5 billion yuan.

The listing makes it the first 12-inch wafer manufacturer developed and brought to mass production in Guangdong province, where it has helped the region break from zero in 12-inch wafer manufacturing.

The company plans to use the 7.5 billion yuan raised on a third 12-inch production phase focused on analog specialty processes, plus research projects for specialty process platforms.

CanSemi focuses on analog and mixed-signal nodes from 180nm down to 55nm, reported 2025 revenue of 2.58 billion yuan, and ran its fabs at 96.38% utilization last year.

Planned capacity totals 80,000 wafers per month across its two existing fabs, with a third fab of 40,000 wafers per month under construction, which would bring the combined total to 120,000 wafers per month.

Microsoft Unveils Azure Maia 300, Its Third-Generation In-House AI Chip on TSMC 2nm

Microsoft has unveiled Azure Maia 300, its third-generation in-house AI inference chip, with the compute chiplet built on TSMC's 2nm process.

Each accelerator pairs eight HBM4 stacks for 288GB of memory and 18TB/s peak bandwidth, delivering 13.8 PFLOPS of FP4 compute at 950W TDP.

Compared with Maia 200, memory bandwidth is 2.5 times higher, capacity is up 33%, and rack-level token throughput reaches up to 7 times higher.

A full rack holds 96 accelerators at 167kW, providing 27.6TB of HBM and 1.7PB/s of aggregate memory bandwidth, and racks scale into a Superpod of up to 12,288 accelerators over a unified electrical-and-optical network.

CEO Satya Nadella called it the company's biggest bet on in-house silicon to date, saying Maia will expand to gigawatt-scale deployments starting next year as the primary inference system for its own products and services.

Nanya Posts Record Q3 as DRAM Prices Surge Over 60%, Net Profit Jumps 5,474%

Nanya Technology reported record third-quarter results as DRAM average selling prices surged more than 60% quarter on quarter.

Revenue reached NT$133.6 billion, up 611.7% year on year, with gross margin at a record 85.7% and net profit of NT$87.6 billion — a 5,474% year-on-year jump and earnings per share of NT$25.40.

General manager Pei-Ying Lee said AI-driven demand has displaced conventional memory supply, forcing some customers to downgrade specs or even use recycled DRAM, and expects prices to keep rising in the fourth quarter with further gross-margin upside.

AI-infrastructure applications now contribute over 20% of revenue, and AI-ASIC memory projects with Western customers have begun small-volume shipments.

The company also confirmed its 5A fab will begin equipment move-in in the first quarter of 2027, the largest single DRAM investment in Taiwan in nearly two decades at up to NT$346.6 billion (about US$10.7 billion).
