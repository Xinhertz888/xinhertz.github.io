Alibaba's T-Head Unveils Automotive-Grade XuanTie E908A, Maps Out Full RISC-V Ecosystem Push

Alibaba's T-Head (DAMO Academy) released the XuanTie E908A, a new automotive-grade-enhanced processor built on the E908 platform, billed as the first processor to simultaneously reach ASIL-B/ASIL-D levels, with performance leading contemporary rivals by 20% and single-point fault metric (SPFM) exceeding 90% for the first time in the industry. It provides a real-time compute foundation for safety islands, domain controllers and in-vehicle gateways.

The roadmap calls for C925A, C930A and C950A to follow, with the C950A expected to deliver 5x the combined performance of the first generation; server products based on the newest XuanTie 950 are expected to appear next year, and T-Head projects single-core multi-threaded SPECint2026 to surpass 100 points by 2028.

On the hardware-ecosystem side, the new XT-LINK high-performance multi-core interconnect keeps XuanTie C950 efficiency at about 78% with 128 cores; for four agentic-AI workload scenarios, multi-core XuanTie C950 systems lead x86 rivals by about 11%, while joint SMT exploration with ZTE Microelectronics delivers a 35% performance gain on SMT2 (C950).

The AI-native XPU architecture natively supports scalar, vector and matrix (tensor) compute with typical operator utilization above 80%, multi-level memory (SRAM-system cache-DDR) with bandwidth utilization above 90%, and Transformer-targeted SFU hotspot acceleration that improves Softmax, Norm and RoPE operators by 2-10x — with Qwen3.8-Flash-Next inference reaching 43 tokens per second.

Software contributions are piling up: 43 GCC patches (17.5% community share, second only to Qualcomm's 34.6%, worth about 10% typical performance gain), 111 LLVM patches (+20% versus community LLVM), and 48 OpenJDK patches with the Chinese Academy of Sciences' Institute of Software (56.47% share, +31% SPECjbb on the C950). A 20-board A210 cloud cluster now runs daily AOSP builds and CTS/VTS regression tests, and commercial OS editions are shipping with Ubuntu, Alibaba Cloud and Kylinsoft.

Shenzhen Packaging-Equipment 'Little Giant' Weijian Files for IPO Tutoring

Shenzhen Weijian Advanced Packaging Technology Co., founded in December 2019 by chairman Lei Weizhuang, completed IPO tutoring registration with the Shenzhen securities regulator on October 19, with Huatai United Securities as the tutoring broker. It remains at the tutoring stage, with no audited financial data disclosed and no listing venue named.

The national-level 'little giant' SME builds advanced packaging equipment: storage-chip packaging gear such as the MV-M50, capable of 16-layer stacking for HBM-class high-density memory, and optical-transceiver packaging equipment including couplers supporting dual-lens, dual-FA, CPO lens and FAU coupling, plus 1.5-micron high-precision die bonders and TCB equipment.

Business is surging: first-half sales of storage packaging equipment grew more than 500% year on year and optical-transmission packaging equipment more than 300%; the company serves seven to eight of the world's top ten optical device and module makers, with overseas orders accounting for more than 20% of the total.

In May it expanded its manufacturing headquarters to over 20,000 square meters, lifting annual capacity past 3,000 units with one-to-three-month delivery on standard models, and a second round of capacity expansion is underway.

Funding history runs one round per year: Pre-A in August 2021 (tens of millions, led by SMIC Juyuan), A in April 2022 (tens of millions, led by BaseStone Capital), A+ in October 2023 (near RMB 100 million, led by Haitong Kaiyuan), A++ in January 2024, and B in August 2025 (over RMB 100 million). There is no controlling shareholder; the three founders controlled 65.72% of voting rights as of end-June.

Tianyue Advanced Swings to Profit as 8-Inch SiC Substrates Top 50% of Revenue

China's Tianyue Advanced reported first-nine-months revenue of RMB 1.559 billion, up 40.22% year on year to a record high, with net profit of RMB 27.22 million — up 2,330.24% year on year. The third quarter alone delivered RMB 646 million in revenue and RMB 85.83 million in net profit, erasing a roughly RMB 58.6 million first-half loss; Q3 gross margin recovered to 27.46%.

Revenue from 8-inch SiC substrates passed 50% of the total, which the report notes puts the domestic maker on the same starting line as global leaders — a step from being able to make large wafers to making them at scale and at low cost.

The company attributes the inflection to rising 800V EV-platform penetration driving automotive-grade conductive substrates, with AI-server high-voltage power emerging as a second growth engine alongside PV/energy storage and ultra-fast charging. Net margin remains thin at about 1.75%, and capex stays heavy: operating cash flow was RMB 89 million while investing outflows exceeded RMB 2.6 billion.

Xinde Semiconductor Clears HKEX Hearing, Eyes Advanced-Packaging Expansion

Hong Kong's stock exchange disclosed on October 21 that Jiangsu Xinde Semiconductor Technology has passed its listing hearing. The OSAT firm develops packaging design, custom packaging products and testing services through its "CAPiC" platform, spanning QFN, BGA, LGA, WLP and 2.5D/3D.

IPO proceeds will fund a new production base and lines, advanced-packaging R&D — specifically LDFO, X-SiP, TXV and 2.5D/3D integration — plus commercialization and customer-ecosystem expansion.

Revenue rose from RMB 509.1 million in 2023 to RMB 827.4 million in 2024 and RMB 1.012 billion in 2025, while the gross loss margin improved from 38.4% to 18.0%. The company holds 225 patents in China, including 39 invention patents, plus three PCT applications.

Resistor Supply Tightens: YAGEO, Walsin Hiked Prices 15–20%, Lead Times Stretch

Current-sense resistors are being repriced from supporting parts to system-critical components as a passive-components shortage cycle takes hold. YAGEO raised prices on key resistor series by 15–20% and Walsin raised its full resistor lineup by about 20% in February 2026, citing elevated silver, palladium, ruthenium, tin and copper costs; smaller makers followed with 5–20% increases.

Lead times for high-end parts have stretched: YAGEO resistors sit around 24 weeks and Samsung Electro-Mechanics around 44 weeks, versus about 10 weeks for ROHM on its IDM supply; new capacity is slow to release as line construction and customer qualification cycles are long.

Demand tracks electrification and higher power: NEV battery management, onboard chargers and inverters, industrial servo and drives, and — the fastest-growing segment — AI/data-center server power supplies chasing higher power density and smaller packages.
