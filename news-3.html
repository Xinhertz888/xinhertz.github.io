Auto Industry Faces Cost 'High Wall' as Memory Prices Spike Post-Holiday

China's auto supply chain is facing across-the-board cost hikes after the Golden Week holiday: more than 60 tire makers issued over 70 price-increase letters since September (2%-5%), carbon black prices rose about 45% within a single month, and rubber futures hit a nine-year high.

Memory chips are automakers' biggest pain point: TrendForce reports global conventional DRAM contract prices rose 90%-95% quarter-on-quarter in Q1 2026, while automotive-grade storage purchase prices jumped about 180% between March and June as AI demand crowds out automotive memory capacity.

UBS estimates memory inflation alone adds RMB 3,000-7,000 to the cost of a high-end ADAS vehicle; NIO said its per-vehicle cost rose roughly RMB 14,000 in Q2 versus end-2025, with memory accounting for about RMB 10,000 of that.

Automakers are fighting back with long-term agreements (GM and Ford both signed LTAs with Micron in July), multi-source battery strategies, and material substitution such as aluminum-for-copper; industry profit still fell 16% year-on-year in January-August with a sales margin of just 3.6%.

The article notes China's automotive chip localization rate topped roughly 35% in the first half of 2026, but high-end compute SoCs and high-safety MCUs remain under 5% localized.

Domestic Brands Take Over 60% of China's Servo Market as Industrial 'Chip' Momentum Builds

China's 2025 general servo market reached RMB 22.96 billion, with domestic brands topping 60% share for the first time (versus under 50% five years ago), while Japanese brands fell from over 50% before 2020 to about 26%, according to GeWu Zhisheng's market white paper.

Low-voltage inverters tell a similar story: the 2025 market was about RMB 29.27 billion, with local brands climbing to 46% and breaking the long-standing ABB/Siemens dominance.

Invosun (汇川技术) holds roughly 22% servo share as the domestic No.1, ahead of Panasonic and Yaskawa, while StarPower Semiconductor earned Invosun's 'strategic supplier' honor in January 2026 with custom-developed IGBT and SiC modules.

Local power-device makers are evolving from build-to-spec suppliers into co-definition partners: Silan Micro is advancing an 'MCU + power module + driver IC' integrated portfolio, and MacroMicro is building a 'chip + module' platform across new energy, EVs, and data centers.

Caveats remain: high-end IGBT modules, precision SiC devices, and high-end IPM modules still lag by a generation and depend heavily on imports, while wide-bandgap cost barriers are slowing large-scale penetration.

CW Lasers Emerge as the Tightest Link in the CPO Supply Chain

As CPO architecture moves the light source out of the module, a single ultra-high-power CW laser must deliver 400mW of output fiber power and hold it at 55°C or higher; per Coherent's ECOC 2026 architecture, one external light source (ELS) packs up to 8 UHP CW lasers, each split across roughly four links.

Only Lumentum and Coherent publicly supply 400mW-class devices: Nvidia invested $2 billion in each in March 2026 with multi-year purchase commitments, and Coherent said indium phosphide remains its main internal constraint even as June-quarter InP laser output rose about 80% year-on-year.

Domestic vendors are 'delivered at the low end, qualifying at the high end': Yuanjie ships 70mW and 100mW CW lasers in volume (first-half revenue of RMB 925 million, up 351.4% year-on-year, with net profit up 1,212.2%), while its 120mW/300mW/400mW chips are still in qualification; Shijia Photonics ships non-cooled 100mW CW DFB in volume and room-temperature 400mW CW DFB in small volume.

Sanan Optoelectronics plans 400mW sampling for 3.2T optical switches in Q4 2026 with volume shipments in Q1 2027; Accelink and Hisense's Nazhen have high-power models in customer qualification, and Accelink is investing about RMB 6.75 billion toward 800G/1.6T/3.2T capacity.

The article concludes that the real race is not lighting up 400mW in the lab, but ramping yield and capacity at the same time once qualification clears.

Domestic Five-Phase Stepper Motors Challenge Long-Standing Japanese Dominance

China's 2025 stepper market exceeded RMB 3.2 billion, with industrial steppers at about RMB 2 billion (up 5% year-on-year), per MIR; five-phase steppers — prized for smaller step angles and smoother motion — were long dominated by Japan's Oriental Motor PK/PKP series.

Founded in 2015, Ruite Technology launched a 20mm-flange five-phase motor in 2026 with a 0.72° step angle, filling the hard-to-develop small-frame gap and now covering 20/28/42/60mm — one of the most complete domestic lineups.

Side-by-side repeat-positioning tests of Ruite's 28CM005-D V3.0 against a leading Japanese product on the same drive and platform showed comparable precision.

Ruite's high-performance five-phase solutions are already in volume use on 400G, 800G, and 1.6T optical-module coupling machines, achieving ±0.05μm repeat positioning on XYZ axes in 12-axis FA coupling systems.

The article frames this as domestic steppers moving from single-model replacement toward full product-line coverage of the precision segment.

Skyworks Closes $22 Billion Qorvo Merger, Creating RF Powerhouse

Skyworks Solutions has completed its previously announced combination with Qorvo, a cash-and-stock transaction valued at approximately $22 billion when it was first announced in October 2025; the deal closed on October 5, 2026 after clearing all regulatory approvals.

Under the terms, Qorvo shareholders received $32.50 in cash plus 0.960 of a Skyworks share for each Qorvo share, leaving legacy Skyworks holders with roughly 63% of the combined company and former Qorvo holders with about 37% on a fully diluted basis.

The combined business keeps the Skyworks name and the SWKS ticker on Nasdaq, and brings together around 8,000 engineers and more than 12,000 issued and pending patents, adding RF GaN, low-voltage power and wired broadband to the portfolio.

Skyworks said the merger more than doubles its addressable market, extending beyond smartphones into defense and aerospace, data center and networking, automotive, edge IoT and AI infrastructure.

The company expects at least $500 million in annualized cost synergies within 24 to 36 months and said the transaction is immediately accretive to non-GAAP earnings per share; part of the cash consideration was financed with $2 billion in senior notes issued in August 2026.
