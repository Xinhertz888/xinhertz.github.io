Samsung Plans 2027 Entry Into Silicon Photonics Foundry, Eyes Full CPO by 2029

Samsung plans to enter silicon photonics in 2027, starting with 12-inch wafer foundry services for photonic integrated circuits and later expanding into optoelectronic integration and optical packaging, South Korea's MTN reported on October 16. The company is also considering acquisitions to fill out its technology portfolio.

The roadmap calls for a thermocompression-bonded optical engine in 2027, hybrid copper bonding in 2028, and a complete co-packaged optics (CPO) offering in 2029. Samsung first disclosed its silicon photonics foundry ambitions at OFC in March 2026, showing a 300mm-wafer PDK whose silicon modulator was independently verified by imec at 224Gbps per channel — enough for 800G (4-channel) or 1.6T (8-channel) optical modules.

Samsung has reportedly purchased probe stations from FormFactor and MPI to build in-house photonics wafer test capability and has secured an order from a major optical transceiver module maker. Yole Group forecasts the optical transceiver market growing from about $10 billion in 2021 to $112 billion in 2031, a CAGR of roughly 35%.

The foundry contest remains lopsided: TrendForce put TSMC's share of top-ten foundry revenue at 72.5% in Q2 2026 versus 5.9% for Samsung, whose COUPE platform is seen as the CPO ecosystem reference. GlobalFoundries bought Singapore's Advanced Micro Foundry in November 2025 to become the largest pure-play silicon photonics foundry by revenue, while UMC licensed imec's iSiPP in December 2025.

Alibaba's T-Head XuanTie Surpasses 6 Billion RISC-V Cores Shipped

DAMO Academy's XuanTie (T-Head) said its cumulative RISC-V chip shipments have exceeded 6 billion units, spanning smart devices and server-adjacent applications, in a story carried by eefocus on October 20.

Its flagship C950, released in March 2026, runs at 3.2GHz and scores over 70 in single-core SPECint2006 — above 22 points per GHz — a record for RISC-V. Through the XT-LINK multicore interconnect, C950 achieves over 75% linear scaling. The team claims more than 10% performance advantages over mainstream incumbent architectures on four types of agentic AI workloads.

At the 2026 RISC-V China Summit in Shenzhen, XuanTie launched the automotive-enhanced E908A IP — the industry's first RISC-V CPU IP meeting both ASIL-B and ASIL-D, with single-point fault metric (SPFM) above 90% — and outlined the C950A, an automotive version targeting five times the performance of existing automotive RISC-V products, expected by the end of 2027.

Ecosystem momentum: the team is the second-largest contributor to GCC's RISC-V code globally and contributed more than half of OpenJDK's RISC-V code; DAMO expert Li Chunqiang said simultaneous multithreading is yielding 30% interim performance gains, with single-core multithreaded SPECint2006 targeted to exceed 100 points by 2028. SHD Research estimates automotive RISC-V SoC revenue at $1.84 billion in 2025, rising to $12.3 billion in 2031 at a 37.3% CAGR.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics Signs 360.5 Billion Won Package Substrate Supply Deal

Samsung Electro-Mechanics disclosed in a regulatory filing on October 19 that it has signed a 360.5 billion won (about $250 million) contract with a global major to supply semiconductor package substrates for storage devices. It is the company's first disclosed long-term agreement (LTA) in the package substrate segment, according to laoyaoba.

The deal brings Samsung Electro-Mechanics' disclosed LTAs this year to seven — the rest concentrated in components such as silicon capacitors, MLCCs and inductors — with cumulative contracted value of roughly 4.3 trillion won.

The company produces FC-BGA and BGA substrates through its package solutions division; industry observers attribute the deal to growing demand for high-value AI server GPU/CPU substrates and chiplet-based heterogeneous integration.

Last month the company unveiled investment plans of 4.27 trillion won for its FC-BGA facility in Sejong, South Korea and 2.51 trillion won for manufacturing equipment in Vietnam; including the Vietnam plant expansion, total spending is expected to exceed 8 trillion won.

Dry Descum Duopoly: Lam Holds 40%, China's ACM at 33.7%, Says Jiwei Report

集微咨询 (Jiwei Consulting) released its 2026 descum-equipment industry report on October 20, pegging the global dry descum equipment market at about $858 million in 2025 and forecasting $1.471 billion by 2032, a CAGR of roughly 8.01%.

The market is a duopoly: CR5 exceeds 90%, with Lam Research leading at 40% and China's ACM Research (屹唐股份) close behind at 33.7%, followed by TEL (15%), PSK (7%) and Hitachi High-Tech (4.3%).

China's descum equipment market (dry plus wet tools) reached about 4.73 billion yuan in 2025, up 18.6% year on year, driven by continued capex from SMIC, Hua Hong, YMTC and CXMT. The domestic-content rate for new dry descum tools on 28nm-and-above lines now exceeds 80%, and ACM's products are in use at global top-ten chipmakers.

Demand intensity rises with process complexity: per-wafer descum cycles grow from about 30 at 28nm to around 70 at 3nm logic, while 3D NAND stacks move from 128 layers toward 300-plus.

South Korean Finance Minister Sees Chip Boom Lasting Through 2027; Maeil Warns of 2029 Memory "Red Ocean"

South Korea's Minister of Economy and Finance Lee Hyoung-il said on October 17, on the sidelines of the IMF–World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok, that the semiconductor boom would last "at least until next year," citing a still-wide gap between supply and demand as supply continues to expand.

SK hynix's second-quarter operating profit surged 557% year on year, and management reiterated that demand will outstrip supply through 2030; CEO Kwak Noh-jung said in August that the memory shortage would persist into 2030. Over the past year, Samsung Electronics shares rose roughly 150% and SK hynix about 250%.

Volatility remains sharp: on October 13 Samsung fell 7.5% intraday to 231,750 won and SK hynix dropped 8.4% as the KOSPI lost nearly 7% to 5,944.31 points, and on October 15 the index plunged 7.57% in a single session. Elon Musk responded on X that China "will produce a lot of RAM in 2028."

South Korea's Maeil Business Newspaper argued in an October 15 column that the period through 2028 is a "safe zone," but warned that new capacity flooding the market from 2029 onward means a memory "red ocean in 2029" and a downturn in 2030. SK hynix reports third-quarter results on October 28 and Samsung on October 29; US September CPI rose 3.7% year on year.

Sources: eet-china.com
