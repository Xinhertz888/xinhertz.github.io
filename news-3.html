Goke Micro Raises Packaged KGD Storage Prices a Second Time This Year

Hunan Goke Microelectronics notified customers and distributors on October 22 of a new round of price increases on packaged KGD (Known Good Die) storage products effective November 2026. It is Goke's second hike this year, and the first quoted as absolute dollar amounts rather than percentage increases.

Under the notice, packaged 64MB KGD rises by $0.50, 128MB by $1.00, and 256MB by $1.50; products with external DDR will be notified separately. The stated reason is the industry-wide DDR shortage and rising market prices widening the supply gap, with substrate and lead-frame material costs also climbing sharply. In January 2026, Goke raised packaged 512Mb, 1Gb and 2Gb KGD by 40%, 60% and 80% respectively using percentage terms.

TrendForce data shows Q4 2026 conventional DRAM contract prices are expected to rise 10-15% quarter-on-quarter, PC DRAM 13-18%, and NAND Flash 15-20%. Packaged KGD, which integrates a controller and tested DRAM dies in one package, is widely used in surveillance, automotive electronics, industrial control, edge AI and IoT devices, where downstream customers have little room to pause purchases.

Jensen Huang Sees No Memory Oversupply Through 2029, Open to Working With CXMT

NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang told CNBC on October 22 during the GTC Berlin conference that despite the past week's sharp selloff in memory stocks on oversupply fears, he does not see memory oversupply emerging through 2029. 'The good thing about memory is you can always use more,' he said, noting that HBM will keep being used for many years to come.

Asked whether NVIDIA would work with China's CXMT and adopt its memory products, Huang said 'we're open,' adding that 'almost every company in the world works with a Chinese company somewhere in their supply chain.' He praised China's manufacturing prowess and acknowledged that U.S. export controls have accelerated China's semiconductor development: 'No doubt, we accelerated all of this.'

The selloff was triggered by CXMT president Cao Kan Yu's October 10 announcement of key progress on 4F² DRAM architecture, targeting DDR5 RDIMM server memory products by the end of 2026. TrendForce data shows CXMT's global DRAM revenue share reached about 9.5% in Q2 2026, ranking fourth worldwide. Micron dismissed 4F² as lacking a viable path to scale after more than two decades of exploration, while Samsung confirmed it is developing a 4F² VCT architecture expected to reach commercial production around 2028.

Micron Dismisses 4F² DRAM as CXMT Advances VSG Transistor, Eyes Server DDR5 RDIMM

ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) has made key progress on its next-generation 4F² DRAM cell architecture and plans to launch server-grade DDR5 RDIMM products combining the 4F² architecture with hybrid-bonding technology before the end of 2026.

At its October 21, 2026 technology webinar, Micron took the opposite view: after more than two decades of exploration, 4F² DRAM has no feasible scaling path due to complexity and cost. Micron is abandoning the 4F² route, extending the advantages of its 6F² architecture and making 3D DRAM its future direction.

CXMT's 4F² architecture uses a self-developed vertical surrounding-gate (VSG) transistor, shrinking cell footprint by about one third and lifting theoretical storage density by about 50%. Founded in 2016 and headquartered in Hefei, CXMT has grown its global DRAM revenue share from around 4% to about 10%, according to HSBC data.

Micron's node roadmap: 1γ is in high-volume production with first EUV use; 1δ (seventh-generation 10nm-class DRAM) is ramping for volume in the second half of 2027 — the first node manufactured in the US — delivering about 25% generational density gain over 1γ; 1ε introduces wafer bonding; the ζÅ (Zeta-Angstrom) first sub-10nm-class node is in early development with High-NA EUV; 3D DRAM will evolve through 3D1 and 3D2 nodes.

NVIDIA Signs Six Suppliers to Massive Capacity Expansion at GTC Berlin

On October 21, 2026, the opening day of GTC Berlin, NVIDIA announced large-scale capacity expansion agreements with six suppliers — Amkor, Ibiden, KYEC, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, SPIL and Unimicron — spanning substrates, advanced packaging, semiconductor testing and optical engines.

Substrates: product substrate capacity will rise 2.5x within three years; NVIDIA is paying Ibiden billions of dollars in capacity-reservation prepayments to expand its Ohno plant toward triple current levels; Samsung Electro-Mechanics joins as a new member, contributing its Korean line capacity immediately while upgrading and building its Sejong and Busan plants.

Advanced packaging: with Amkor, Korea interposer wafer capacity is set to rise more than 300% by end-2028; Amkor signed a new $1.3 billion agreement the same day, bringing its NVIDIA-related agreements to $3 billion.

Testing: total test capacity is expected to double during 2026-2027; NVIDIA is paying KYEC an $1.1 billion prepayment for a new US test facility targeting mass production in April 2027.

Optical engines: with SPIL, Taiwan optical-engine capacity is targeted at 15x current levels by mid-2027 and doubling again by end-2028.

DeepSeek Deepens Huawei Ties, Plans Pre-Training on Ascend 950DT by Year-End

The Information reported on October 20, citing sources, that DeepSeek is working closely with Huawei and participating in design decisions for future Huawei AI chips; Huawei is adjusting chip designs to DeepSeek's requirements on model computation and inter-processor data transfer, with the resulting designs feeding AI chips launching in 2027 and beyond.

DeepSeek plans to begin AI model pre-training on Huawei's Ascend 950DT as early as year-end, with latest tests already using over 1,000 processors — the timeline subject to test progress. Huawei plans to prioritize most of the supply of the Ascend 960DT, launching in Q1 2027, for DeepSeek, for both training and inference.

DeepSeek has open-sourced TileLang compiler tools and compute/communication components for the Ascend platform (TileLang was developed by Peking University researchers), releasing a version supporting the Ascend 950 on September 30. IDC data shows NVIDIA's share of China's datacenter AI-chip market fell to 34% in the first half of 2026, with Huawei at 28% — the gap narrowed to six percentage points.
