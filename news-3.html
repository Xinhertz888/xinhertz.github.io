Axera Launches AX8860 Edge AI SoC Series for Industrial 'Physical AI'

At CIIF 2026, Chinese chipmaker Axera launched the AX8860 series of large-compute edge AI SoCs, targeting industrial intelligence as AI moves from the cloud to the physical world. The series includes the AX8860 and AX8860P, positioned as compute platforms 'born for physical AI.'

Built on a 6nm process after 15 months of development, the chips deliver up to 256 TOPS with mixed-precision compute and are among the industry's first edge chips natively supporting FP4 precision. Each chip carries 273 GB/s of memory bandwidth and natively supports 128GB of LPDDR5X; a built-in AXLink chip-to-chip interface enables dual- and quad-chip cascading with memory pooling, reaching 512GB of memory with four chips — enough to run models above 100 billion parameters, such as DeepSeek V4 Flash, at the edge.

Axera says early AX8860P tests show prefill and decode speeds matching leading international flagship GPUs, while full-load power consumption is only 45% of the competitor's, with multi-chip cascading efficiency holding around 80%. The launch is accompanied by four hardware forms: the A256S core module, PCIe inference cards (single, dual and quad-chip; the quad-chip card offers roughly 1000 TOPS and a 512GB memory pool), the Agentic Box, and edge inference servers.

RISC-V Funding Wave: Ruisixin Closes New Round as Sector Eyes Consolidation

Chinese RISC-V server-chip startup Ruisixin has closed a new funding round with Renjun Capital and Shuimu Fund participating, according to a report (amount undisclosed). The team comes from the original Berkeley RISC-V project group, and founder Tan Zhangxi studied under RISC-V co-founder David Patterson.

The round caps a dense financing streak across the sector: Lanxin Computing closed a Series B of nearly 2 billion yuan with 18 investors last month, Lingrui Zhixin raised several hundred million yuan in July, and last year Jindie Shikong, Chaorui Technology and Yuefang Technology each closed nine-figure rounds. Overseas, Tenstorrent raised a $693 million Series D.

Policy support is also crystallizing: the 15th Five-Year Plan for electronic information manufacturing issued by MIIT and NDRC lists 'advancing 5th-generation RISC (RISC-V) R&D and industrialization' — the first time RISC-V has appeared as a task item in a national five-year plan. The article notes industry-wide consolidation may be next, as competition shifts from single demos to ecosystem completeness and real deployments.

3D-Architecture AI Chip Startup Suanmiao Raises Nearly 2 Billion Yuan in a Year

Chinese AI cloud-compute chip startup Suanmiao Technology announced it has closed its Pre-A2 and A funding rounds, bringing its total fundraising over the past year to nearly 2 billion yuan, according to the report.

The company focuses on R&D and volume production of large-compute AI cloud chips built on a 3D architecture. It says proceeds are earmarked for three priorities: recruiting top R&D talent, doubling down on core-technology iteration, and accelerating the mass-production ramp of its self-developed 3D TokenPU large-model compute chip.

Rather than following the conventional GPU route, Suanmiao is betting its 3D chip architecture can carve out a differentiated path in AI inference compute.

Sanan Optoelectronics' Controlling Shareholders Face New Bankruptcy-Restructuring Filings

Sanan Optoelectronics (600703.SH) disclosed on the evening of October 15 that creditors Hubei Changjiang Anxin Industry Investment Fund and Hunan Lugu Development Group applied to the Xiamen Intermediate People's Court for bankruptcy restructuring of controlling shareholder Xiamen Sanan Electronics; Hunan Lugu Development Group also filed against indirect controlling shareholder Fujian Sanan Group.

The court has issued notices for both applications but had not formally accepted them as of the announcement date, so whether the companies enter restructuring remains uncertain.

The two shareholders together hold 1.312 billion shares, or 26.30% of total share capital, of which 794 million shares (60.52%) are pledged; all 1.312 billion shares are judicially frozen, with cumulative round-robin freezes of 8.213 billion shares — 625.89% of their holdings and 164.63% of total share capital.

The company said operations remain normal; H1 2026 revenue was RMB 6.468 billion, down 28.04% year-on-year, with a net loss of RMB 97.9 million. Founder Lin Xiucheng was placed under criminal detention on September 24 on suspicion of embezzlement and misappropriation of funds.

Sanan closed at RMB 10.78 on October 15, down 3.66%, for a market cap of about RMB 54.1 billion. This follows an earlier June 2026 filing by creditor Lin Suzhen, which was withdrawn in September.

Oct 16, 2026

Japan to Fund US$21 Billion Twin 300mm Fabs in New York, GlobalFoundries to Operate Them

GlobalFoundries announced on October 14 that it was selected as technology provider and operator for two new 300mm fabs at its Malta, New York site, fully funded by US$21 billion of Japanese investment — the first major semiconductor manufacturing project under the US-Japan trade and investment framework.

Funding flows from the July 22, 2025 US-Japan agreement under which Japan committed US$550 billion in US investment with tariffs capped at 15%.

About US$10 billion will accelerate the first fab, targeting groundbreaking in Q4 2026, tool-install readiness in 2028, and first wafers in 2029; another roughly US$10 billion supports the second fab.

At full capacity the two fabs add roughly 1.8 million 300mm wafers per year, lifting total US wafer capacity by about 10%, and are projected to create some 27,000 construction and ecosystem jobs plus over 1,000 skilled manufacturing jobs.

GlobalFoundries itself puts up no capital: the US and Japanese governments will hold the plants and equipment through an SPV, while GF earns construction, operating, and licensing fees; CFO Sam Franklin expects about US$3 billion of cumulative free cash flow over ten years from the first fab and long-term EPS accretion of US$0.50 to US$0.75.

The US Commerce Department will reallocate US$955 million of CHIPS and Science Act funding to accelerate GF's silicon photonics, silicon-germanium, and advanced packaging investments in Malta and Burlington, Vermont, and separately takes about 4% equity in GlobalFoundries.

Endorsements came from Renesas CEO Hidetoshi Shibata, Apple COO Sabih Khan (on the back of Apple's US$600 billion US investment pledge), GM President Mark Reuss, Lynx Logic, and Tokyo Electron.
