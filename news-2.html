Dell CEO Warns AI Server Memory Shortage to Worsen Into 2027

Dell Technologies CEO Michael Dell has warned that tight supply of HBM and DRAM will worsen further in 2027, as AI infrastructure demand keeps outpacing global memory capacity growth — making memory the key bottleneck for the AI server industry.

Dell currently sits on a record $95 billion backlog of enterprise and AI server orders, but converting those orders into revenue hinges on securing enough memory chips.

According to The Wall Street Journal, Dell believes the supply constraint could last up to four years, since new fabs take years from planning to full production — much of the added capacity is not expected to be fully online until 2029 or 2030.

To limit risk, Dell runs a lean-inventory model — buying DRAM close to final assembly to avoid tying up working capital — while locking in supply through long-term vendor agreements and customer prepayments.

Rising memory prices are also pushing up manufacturing costs for AI servers and other high-end systems, adding pressure across the AI supply chain.

XHEART Closes Strategic Round, Lifetime Funding Reaches ~5 Billion Yuan

Chinese physical-AI compute chip startup XHEART (新芯航途) completed a strategic funding round on October 16, bringing its cumulative financing to approximately 5 billion yuan.

Renault Group joined as a first-time investor, Chery Auto increased its stake, and domestic memory leader CXMT (Changxin Memory) also participated in the round.

XHEART builds dedicated compute for edge-side physical-AI models. Its flagship product, the X7 ADAS large-model chip launched in March, uses a dedicated NPU with a hardware-software co-design architecture targeting 200+ TOPS for L2++ urban driving assistance and L3/L4 autonomous driving.

The X7 debuted on the SAIC Volkswagen ID.ERA 9X and is entering the supply systems of SAIC's MG and Roewe brands, GM's Cadillac, and selected Chery models. The company's largest shareholder traces back to Momenta founder Cao Xudong.

VTest Wins Approval for 2 Billion Yuan Convertible Bond to Expand High-End Chip Test Capacity

Shanghai VTest Semiconductor (伟测科技) has received SSE approval to issue up to 2 billion yuan in convertible bonds, with proceeds earmarked for expanding test capacity for AI compute chips, memory chips, and high-end CIS.

The funds target two projects: the Shanghai HQ base (700 million yuan of the raise) and the Southwest HQ and R&D test base in Chengdu (800 million yuan), both involving new land, facilities, and high-end test equipment.

VTest, a leading third-party IC test house, covers wafer test, final test, SLT, and burn-in for AI chips, CPUs, GPUs, DPUs, SoCs, FPGAs, MCUs, memory, RF, and high-end CIS devices, serving over 200 customers including Unisoc, ZTE Microelectronics, GigaDevice, Fudan Microelectronics, SMIC, Rockchip, and Novosense.

The company reported H1 2026 revenue of 1.072 billion yuan, up 69% year on year, with net profit of 159 million yuan, up 57.6%, driven by high-end test demand from AI compute, smart driving, and large-capacity memory chips.

This is VTest's second straight year of convertible-bond financing, following an 1.175 billion yuan issue in April 2025 that was redeemed in March 2026 after a share-price rally.

Jialichuang Plans Hong Kong Listing Less Than Three Months After A-Share Debut

Jialichuang (嘉立创, 001232) announced plans to issue H shares and list on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange main board — less than three months after its A-share debut on August 4, when its shares peaked above 234 yuan and market value once exceeded 130 billion yuan.

The company, a one-stop electronics-industry service provider covering PCB manufacturing, electronic component distribution, and assembly, says the listing would support its globalization strategy, widen offshore financing channels, and strengthen its overseas brand.

Overseas revenue grew 63.76% year on year to 1.516 billion yuan in H1 2026, accounting for 21.14% of total revenue. The company also guided Q1–Q3 2026 net profit to rise 78.76%–118.48% year on year, partly on overseas growth.

In the same roundup, Dongshan Precision received CSRC filing approval to issue up to 234,038,600 H shares for a Hong Kong listing, moving its IPO process to the HKEX hearing and pricing stage.

Changfei Advanced Launches SiC MOSFET Series, New Power Modules at Shenzhen Tech Event

SiC power semiconductor maker Changfei Advanced, founded in May 2022, held a "Everest Summit" technology launch in Shenzhen on October 15, unveiling its high-performance SiC MOSFET series PG4E/PG5E/PG6N, E2Drive automotive main-drive power modules, DeepXE embedded-PCB power modules, and EZ2C/3C — described as China's first diffusion-welded SiC power modules — alongside new products for AI data centers, industrial, and consumer markets.

The company said its portfolio has grown from zero to 131 products, with process cell size shrunk from 5.8μm to 2.8μm, a 575-person R&D team, and combined annual capacity of 420,000 SiC wafers across its Wuhan and Wuhu fabs. Wuhan is billed as the SiC industry's first A3-level smart wafer fab, with MOSFET production cycle of about 40 days and over 98% CP yield on 5×5mm chips.

On specs: the 1200V PG4E posts room-temperature Rsp of 2.2 mΩ·cm² (4.4 mΩ·cm² at 175°C), 2.1μs short-circuit withstand (27% above rivals), and 11.2% higher avalanche energy. The PG5E cuts room-temperature Rsp to 1.9 mΩ·cm² (about 13.5% below the prior generation) with 30% better short-circuit withstand versus competitors, while the PG6N reaches 1.7 mΩ·cm² Rsp and supports operation up to 200–225°C.

For modules: the E2Dmini-J, co-developed with Jingwei HiRain, delivers 540 Arms peak current (20% above the 450 Arms industry level) with 36% lower stray inductance. DeepXE's embedded-PCB module gets stray inductance down to 0.9 nH (75% below conventional packaging), 20% higher current density, 15% lower thermal resistance, and halves size and weight — 24 SiC MOSFETs on a poker-card-sized footprint reaching 250 kW peak. The diffusion-welded EZ2C/3C replaces tin-based solder (melting point ~500°C vs 230°C) with 2–3x bond strength and thermal conductivity, cutting case-to-sink thermal resistance by 18% and full-load junction temperature by over 25°C; EZ3C pushes a single SST module from 70 kW to over 100 kW and halves 10 kV AI SST cascade stages from 16 to 8.

Automotive SiC chips now number 50-plus models with over 20 million units shipped at a claimed 0 PPM quality record; consumer products span 38 models (650V–800V) with over 100 million units shipped, and industrial products 93 models (650V–3300V) with 15 million shipped. For data centers, its 800V HVDC lineup targets PSU cabinets of 18 kW/30 kW at over 98.5% efficiency and 40–80 kW SST modules. On the trench-MOS roadmap, the first-generation TG1N (1200V, 11 mΩ) is in mass production; the second generation shrinks cell size 27% and on-resistance 16% (mass production in 2026), with a third generation at 1.5μm cell and 1.3 mΩ·cm² Rsp slated for 2027 — an Incopat search put its 79 trench-MOS public patents (Oct 2024–Oct 2025) third worldwide behind Fuji Electric (139) and Infineon (109). The article cites a 2025 global SiC power semiconductor market of about $2.79 billion, projected to reach $21.58 billion by 2034 at 25.52% CAGR.
