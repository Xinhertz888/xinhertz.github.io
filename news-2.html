South Korean Finance Minister Sees Chip Boom Lasting Through 2027; Maeil Warns of 2029 Memory "Red Ocean"

South Korea's Minister of Economy and Finance Lee Hyoung-il said on October 17, on the sidelines of the IMF–World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok, that the semiconductor boom would last "at least until next year," citing a still-wide gap between supply and demand as supply continues to expand.

SK hynix's second-quarter operating profit surged 557% year on year, and management reiterated that demand will outstrip supply through 2030; CEO Kwak Noh-jung said in August that the memory shortage would persist into 2030. Over the past year, Samsung Electronics shares rose roughly 150% and SK hynix about 250%.

Volatility remains sharp: on October 13 Samsung fell 7.5% intraday to 231,750 won and SK hynix dropped 8.4% as the KOSPI lost nearly 7% to 5,944.31 points, and on October 15 the index plunged 7.57% in a single session. Elon Musk responded on X that China "will produce a lot of RAM in 2028."

South Korea's Maeil Business Newspaper argued in an October 15 column that the period through 2028 is a "safe zone," but warned that new capacity flooding the market from 2029 onward means a memory "red ocean in 2029" and a downturn in 2030. SK hynix reports third-quarter results on October 28 and Samsung on October 29; US September CPI rose 3.7% year on year.

Report: Samsung to Use TSMC 3nm for NVIDIA's Custom NVHBM Base Die

South Korea's Seoul Economic Daily reported on October 18 that Samsung Electronics will outsource the base die at the bottom of NVIDIA's custom HBM, "NVHBM," to TSMC on its 3nm process. NVIDIA unveiled NVHBM on August 26, 2026; its key design move is relocating the GPU's (which NVIDIA calls XPU) memory controller into the HBM stack's base die alongside an in-house PHY.

Per the report, NVIDIA requires all three suppliers — Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron — to build the base die on TSMC 3nm while the core die uses sixth-generation 1c DRAM. UBS estimates 2027 HBM demand will grow 91% year-on-year to about 615 billion Gb against only around 546 billion Gb of new supply, leaving a roughly 10% shortfall. Samsung is separately validating base-die production on its own 2nm process.

Caveat: as of publication, NVIDIA, Samsung, TSMC, SK Hynix and Micron had issued no official statements; all details trace to the newspaper's anonymous industry sources. SK Hynix previously held the lead NVIDIA HBM supplier position on the strength of its early base-die work.

Zhongxingwei Launches 12 Production Interconnect Chips, China's First PCIe 6.0 Switch

At its October 15 "brand and full-stack product" launch in Wuxi, Chinese interconnect chipmaker Zhongxingwei (众星微) unveiled 12 already-mass-produced high-performance interconnect chips with supporting core IP, plus three next-generation products: a PCIe 6.0 switch, an 800G multi-protocol AI NIC, and a multi-mode RAID chip.

The company frames the AI compute bottleneck as "transport capacity": in large-model training, cluster compute utilization runs as low as just over 20% and at most 53%, while interconnect chips account for 8–9% of system value in GB300- and VR200-class super-nodes. China's first all-domestic 100,000-card compute cluster adopted six Zhongxingwei chips, deploying roughly 200,000 units, with an in-house 32T InfiniBand switch chip paired with 400G NICs handling intra-cluster traffic.

New products include China's first PCIe 6.0 144-lane switch, with native fabric for high-speed XPU interconnect and CXL 3.1 for memory pooling, and a new multi-mode RAID chip whose core throughput is double that of mainstream international rivals. Its storage-interconnect lines (HBA/RAID/SAS Expander) benchmark against overseas leaders across 50 typical workloads.

On quality: 500 servers and 1,000 boards ran 600,000 hours of continuous on-machine testing; chip customer return rates are below 100 DPPM and board returns below 500 DPPM. Ecosystem coverage spans 15+ cloud platforms, 50+ OS variants, 300+ optical-module models and 7 mainstream CPU platforms.

Fulin Integrated Raises Nearly ¥1B Series B to Expand InP/GaAs Foundry for AI Optical Modules

Putian, Fujian-based compound-semiconductor foundry Fulin Integrated (福联集成, founded 2015) has closed a Series B round of nearly 1 billion yuan, co-led by Shenzhen Capital Group (深创投) and Industrial Securities' investment arm, with Fujian Electronics & Information Group, SMIC Fortune (中芯聚源), a national-level industry fund and others participating. Proceeds will fund phase-two expansion, lifting capacity by more than 50%; the company says it is currently sold out at full utilization.

The round's core draw is its 4-inch indium phosphide (InP) line for optical chips: it has entered the supply chains of the world's top-three optical module makers, serving 800G and next-generation 1.6T modules. Its other line is 6-inch gallium arsenide for RF chips — handset power amplifiers, Wi-Fi front ends, satellite communications — serving tier-one customers at home and abroad.

Market context cited in the coverage: global InP substrate shipments are estimated at about 910,000 wafers in 2025 rising to about 1.28 million in 2027, and Lumentum forecasts an 85% CAGR in AI-driven InP optical-channel shipments. In GaAs foundry, Taiwan's WIN Semiconductors holds over 60% of the merchant market at 43,000 wafers a month, while Sanan IC is China's largest at 18,000 a month; Wi-Fi 7 is lifting PA content per handset from 3–4 to 8–10 units.

The company is listed among Fujian's key IPO-candidate cultivation enterprises, and data from Cailian Press cited in the article puts the probability of follow-on financing within two years at around 77%, signaling clear IPO intent.

Changfei Advanced Discloses Diffusion-Bonded SiC Modules, A3-Grade Fab at Baychip Expo

At the 2026 Bay Area Semiconductor Industry Ecosystem Expo (Baychip Expo) in Shenzhen, SiC power semiconductor maker Changfei Advanced disclosed fresh progress on high-performance SiC MOSFETs, PCB-embedded packaging and its A3-grade wafer fab, in an interview with Li Gangqun, vice president of its strategic operations center.

The EZ family is China's first power module line built on an advanced diffusion-bonding process, targeting AI data centers (AIDC) and solid-state transformers (SST). Diffusion-bonded joints deliver 2–3x higher bonding strength; the EZ2C cuts junction-to-case thermal resistance by 18% and full-load die junction temperature by more than 25°C, while supporting operation above 200°C. The EZ3C uses an efficient SHB three-level topology to push a single SST module above 100 kW, halving the cascade stages of a 10 kV AI SST system from 16 to 8.

On trench devices, the company started its trench MOSFET patent program in 2023; double-side deep masking/burial techniques plus rounded trench-bottom processing cut trench sidewall roughness (Ra) to 0.15 nm versus 0.7 nm for rivals. As of October 2025 it held 79 public patents in the field, ranking third worldwide; first- and second-generation devices are designated TG1N and TG2N.

Its Wuhan base is the first SiC wafer fab in China to deploy an A3-grade AMHS overhead-transport and dispatch system, with an automatic wafer-start rate above 98% and smart dispatch above 90%. CP yield on 5 mm × 5 mm automotive-grade large dice is stable above 98%, new-product tape-in cycles are 3x faster, and an AI-driven fab utilities system cut overall fab energy use by more than 10.5%.
